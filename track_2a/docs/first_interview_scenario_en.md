# First interview scenario — English

This fictional example explores a short, adaptive interview for a young person applying for an apprenticeship. It is a design example, not an implemented feature or an actual Apertus response.

English is used here to illustrate the experience. The intended application languages are German, French, and Italian.

## Student and setting

- **Student:** Alex, 16, preparing for a first apprenticeship interview.
- **Role:** IT apprenticeship at a local company.
- **Experience:** No formal work experience. Alex has helped with a school group project and enjoys solving computer problems at home.
- **Practice goal:** Explain an interest in IT using a concrete example from everyday life.

The coach should use approachable language, ask one question at a time, and allow the student time to think. It should invite examples from school, hobbies, or home without assuming professional experience.

## Welcome

**Coach:** Hi Alex. Let's practise a short part of an interview for an IT apprenticeship. I'll ask you a question and then a follow-up based on your answer. Afterwards, we'll look at one thing you did well and one thing you could improve. You can ask me to rephrase a question or pause at any time. Ready to start?

**Student:** Yes.

## Opening question

**Coach:** What interests you about this IT apprenticeship?

## Path A — The student needs help finding an example

**Student:** I like computers, and I think IT could be interesting. I'm not really sure what else to say.

**Coach:** You mentioned that you like computers. Can you tell me about one time at school or at home when you enjoyed doing something with a computer?

**Student:** Our group had to make a presentation for school. Some pictures wouldn't display properly, so I tried different file formats and got them working. I liked figuring out what was wrong.

### Feedback after the exchange

**Coach:** You gave a useful example: when the pictures in your school presentation didn't work, you tried different file formats and found a solution. That helps explain what you enjoy about working with computers.

One way to strengthen your opening answer is to include that example straight away and connect it to what you want to learn in the apprenticeship. You could use this structure: “I'm interested in IT because … For example … During an apprenticeship, I'd like to learn …”

Would you like to try your opening answer again using your own words?

## Path B — The student already gives a specific example

**Student:** I enjoy figuring out why something isn't working. In a school group project, some pictures wouldn't display in our presentation. I tried different file formats until they worked. That made me interested in learning how to solve more technical problems, and I'd like to find out how an IT team does that.

**Coach:** When you were trying different file formats, how did you decide what to try next?

**Student:** At first I just guessed. Then I compared a picture that worked with one that didn't and noticed they had different formats. I converted one picture to test it before changing the others.

### Feedback after the exchange

**Coach:** You connected your interest in IT to a concrete school experience. Your follow-up also explained your own actions: you compared two pictures and tested a change on one before applying it to the others. That gives the interviewer a clear example of how you approached the problem.

To develop your answer further, add one sentence about what this taught you or what you would like to learn next. Choose something that is true for you; you don't need to use technical terms to make it convincing.

Would you like to try again with that extra sentence?

## What this example should help us test

- **Adaptation:** Does the follow-up respond to what the student actually said? Path A helps the student find an example; Path B explores an example already given.
- **Grounded feedback:** Does the coach refer to evidence in the answer without inventing strengths, experience, or outcomes?
- **Useful guidance:** Does the student receive one specific improvement they can try immediately?
- **Supportive tone:** Does the coach treat limited work experience and hesitation as normal, without judging the student's personality or employability?
- **Student ownership:** Does the coach help the student express their own experience rather than supply achievements or a script to memorise?
- **Interview flow:** Does the coach ask one question at a time and save coaching feedback until the short practice exchange ends?

## First test when Apertus access is available

Use each opening answer as a separate test input with the same student context and opening question. Ask Apertus to generate one follow-up, then compare it against the criteria above. The wording does not need to match this example.

Next, provide the corresponding follow-up answer and ask for feedback containing one evidence-based strength, one actionable improvement, and an invitation to retry. If the model asks a different follow-up, write an appropriate fictional answer before testing feedback.

This is a small qualitative starting point. It does not yet test a full interview, language support, dialects, or speech.
